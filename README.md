## The challenge

### Learn. Work. Together.

Robots have started to learn the way language models did.
Models that read images and text now also move robot arms. They follow instructions in plain words.
Labs share more than a million recorded robot episodes. An open-source arm costs a few hundred euros.

The challenge is to build AI that lets people and robots learn from each other and work side by side.
A robot has to see and understand the place where it works. It has to learn from the people who work there.
Those people decide what it records and what it does. Each team picks one track and builds a working prototype.

Each team picks one of eight tracks:

| Axis | Track |
|---|---|
| Ingestion | Video, hands and voice of the person at work |
| Ontology | Steps, tools and checks of a job |
| Memory | Every way a job has been done |
| Visualisation | Instructions a new colleague can follow |
| Decision | Spotting a wrong step as it happens |
| Privacy | The worker decides what is recorded |
| Evaluation | A benchmark of steps and mistakes |
| Repackaging | Training data for robots, with the worker's consent |

## How it works

- **Before.** Before the event you get the challenge and, for your track, the data, the benchmarks and the papers to read.
- **On site.** You work in a team on one track. At midday the team pitches its idea to the jury. If it gets through, it spends the afternoon building the prototype and presents it.
- **Afterwards.** The jury picks the most promising projects. KVA takes them into its accelerator.
