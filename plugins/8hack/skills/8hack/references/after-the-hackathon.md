# AFTER — could this continue?

Most hackathon projects stop on the day, and winning is not what makes them continue. A survey of
participants in 48-hour hackathons found that about one in five founded a startup afterwards, and the
only significant predictor was the participants' entrepreneurial motivation; winning an award was not
([survey of 105 participants, Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-53227-6_15) —
a small sample from one institution's events of 2015–2019: read it as a mechanism, not a number).

So the question at the end of the day is not "did we win?" but "do we want to know whether this is
true, and what is the cheapest way to find out next week?"

## 1. Where the project actually is

Place it honestly on this ladder; most hackathon projects end at level 2 or 3.

| Level | Name | What exists | Dominant risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Intuition | A note, a metaphor | Excitement |
| 1 | Thesis | A sharp claim, an owner, an alternative | Overreach |
| 2 | Prototype | A working flow on fake or small data | A demo that proves the build, not the need |
| 3 | Experiment | A pre-registered test with a result | Cherry-picked evidence |
| 4 | MVP | Real users, real data, a real decision changed | Custom work disguised as a product |
| 5 | Product | Pricing, onboarding, a repeatable channel | Weak distribution |

## 2. The next-week experiment card (under €500)

```
Hypothesis under test (the riskiest assumption): …
Experiment type: landing page + call to action | concierge (deliver it by hand) | pre-order or deposit |
fake door | ten real interviews | other: …
What we show or do: …
Who we reach, how: … (channel, list, community) · Cost: € … · Duration: … days
Metric (a behaviour, not an opinion): …
Pass/fail threshold, written before the build: pass if … ≥ … by …
Why this threshold: … (benchmark, analogy, or the number that would make the business work)
If it passes, Monday we: …
If it fails, Monday we: … (change the proposition | the segment | the channel | stop)
What AI builds for this test: … · What a human verifies before launch: … (claims, tracking, price, legal basics)
```

Pre-register or the result will be negotiated afterwards.

## 3. Continuation check (answer all five)

1. **Motivation**: who in the team wants to spend the next four weeks on this, by name?
2. **Evidence**: did the riskiest assumption survive a real test, or only a demo?
3. **Access**: can the team reach ten real people in the segment within two weeks?
4. **Ownership**: who owns what was built? Read the event's rules and licences before you reuse the
   code or announce anything.
5. **Shape**: is it a product (repeatable for many), a service (custom each time), or research (a
   question still open)? All three are legitimate; they are different paths.

If 1 or 3 is empty, keep the learning and archive the project with its kill reason. That is a good
outcome, not a failure.

## 4. Leave a repo someone else can run

- `README.md` built from `HACK.md`: problem, UVP, what works, what is mocked, demo video link,
  how to run in three commands, the check set and its score, the next test.
- The licence the event's rules require.
- No secrets in the code or the history; rotate anything that was ever committed.
- The check set and the data sample (if the licence allows it), so the result can be reproduced.
- Push it. Work that stays on a laptop does not exist.
